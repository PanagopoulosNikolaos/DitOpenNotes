# Assignment 02: Applications of Derivatives and Integral Calculus

## Objective
Apply differential calculus to optimization and curve sketching, and apply integral calculus to indefinite integration techniques, definite integrals, and geometric area/volume computations.

---

## Technical Specifications

### Problem 1: Applied Geometric Optimization
An open-top cylindrical storage tank with volume $V_0 = 1000\pi\text{ m}^3$ is to be constructed from sheet metal.
1. Formulate the total surface area $A(r)$ of the open cylinder (base plus lateral wall) as a function of the radius $r$.
2. Determine the critical radius $r^*$ that minimizes the total surface area.
3. Verify that $r^*$ corresponds to a global minimum using the second derivative test.
4. Compute the optimal height $h^*$ and show that $h^* = r^*$.

### Problem 2: Indefinite Integration Techniques
Evaluate the following antiderivatives analytically, showing all substitution and decomposition steps:
1. $\displaystyle \int x^3 e^{-x^2} \, dx$ (Integration by parts after substitution)
2. $\displaystyle \int \frac{2x^2 - x + 4}{x^3 + 4x} \, dx$ (Partial fraction decomposition)
3. $\displaystyle \int \frac{1}{\sqrt{x} (1 + \sqrt{x})^2} \, dx$ ($u$-substitution)

### Problem 3: Definite Integrals and Planar Area
1. Compute the derivative $g'(x)$ of the variable-limit integral function using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (Leibniz Rule):
   $$g(x) = \int_{x^2}^{e^{3x}} \sqrt{1 + t^4} \, dt$$
2. Determine the exact area bounded between the two intersecting parabolas:
   $$y_1 = x^2 - 2x \quad \text{and} \quad y_2 = 4x - x^2$$
3. Calculate the volume of the solid generated by revolving the region bounded by $y = \sqrt{x}$, the line $x = 4$, and the $x$-axis around the $x$-axis.
