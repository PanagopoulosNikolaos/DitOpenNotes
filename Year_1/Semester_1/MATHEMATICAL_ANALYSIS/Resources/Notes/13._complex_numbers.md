# Complex Numbers

Introduction to complex numbers, definition of the imaginary unit $i$, powers of $i$, operations on $\mathbb{C}$, the complex conjugate and its properties, the geometric representation and modulus of a complex number, factoring trinomials with negative discriminant, the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, trigonometric (polar) form, De Moivre's Theorem, and the use of Octave.

---

## 1. Definition and Algebraic Form

Complex numbers extend the set of real numbers by allowing the solution of all polynomial equations.

### The Imaginary Unit $i$
We define a special number $i$ (imaginary unit) such that:
$$
i^2 = -1 \implies i = \sqrt{-1}
$$

### Powers of $i$
The powers of $i$ repeat cyclically every $4$ steps:
- $i^0 = 1$
- $i^1 = i$
- $i^2 = -1$
- $i^3 = i^2 \cdot i = -i$
- $i^4 = 1$, and so on.
To find $i^n$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, perform Euclidean division of $n/4$ and find the remainder $u \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$. Then $i^n = i^u$.

### Algebraic Form (Cartesian Form)
Every complex number $z$ can be uniquely written as:
$$
z = a + bi
$$
where $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$.
- $a = \text{Re}(z)$ (Real part)
- $b = \text{Im}(z)$ (Imaginary part)

---

## 2. Operations and Complex Conjugate

### Operations on $\mathbb{C}$
Let $z_1 = a + bi$ and $z_2 = c + di$:
- **Addition/Subtraction:** $z_1 \pm z_2 = (a \pm c) + (b \pm d)i$
- **Multiplication:**
  $$
  z_1 \cdot z_2 = (a + bi)(c + di) = ac + adi + bci + bdi^2 = (ac - bd) + (ad + bc)i
  $$

### Complex Conjugate
The conjugate of the complex number $z = a + bi$ is denoted by $\bar{z}$ (or $z^*$) and is defined as:
$$
\bar{z} = a - bi
$$
- **Fundamental Property:** Multiplying a complex number by its conjugate always yields a real number:
  $$
  z \cdot \bar{z} = (a + bi)(a - bi) = a^2 + b^2
  $$
- **Division:** To divide two complex numbers, multiply the numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator:
  $$
  \frac{z_1}{z_2} = \frac{z_1 \cdot \bar{z}_2}{z_2 \cdot \bar{z}_2} = \frac{(a+bi)(c-di)}{c^2 + d^2}
  $$

### Modulus
The modulus of $z = a + bi$ represents the geometric distance from its image to the origin of the complex plane:
$$
|z| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} = \sqrt{z \cdot \bar{z}}
$$

---

## 3. Trigonometric Form and De Moivre's Theorem

### Trigonometric (Polar) Form
A complex number $z = x + yi \neq 0$ can be expressed using its modulus $r = |z|$ and principal argument $\theta$ (angle with the positive real axis $Ox$):
$$
z = r(\cos(\theta) + i\sin(\theta))
$$
where:
- $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$
- $\cos(\theta) = \frac{x}{r}, \quad \sin(\theta) = \frac{y}{r}$

### De Moivre's Theorem
Used for finding powers of complex numbers in trigonometric form:
$$
z^n = [r(\cos(\theta) + i\sin(\theta))]^n = r^n (\cos(n\theta) + i\sin(n\theta))
$$
for every $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

---

## 4. Complex Numbers in Octave

In Octave, the imaginary unit is defined automatically as `i` or `j`:
```octave
z1 = 2 + 3i;
z2 = 1 - 2i;
prod_z = z1 * z2;
real_part = real(z1);
imag_part = imag(z1);
abs_val = abs(z1); % modulus
conj_z = conj(z1); % conjugate
```

## Solved Exercises

### Exercise 1: Computing a Power of $i$
**Problem:**
Compute the $102$nd power of $i$.

**Solution:**
1. Divide the exponent $102$ by $4$:
   $$
   102 = 4 \cdot 25 + 2
   $$
2. The remainder of the division is $u = 2$.
3. Therefore:
   $$
   i^{102} = i^2 = -1
   $$

### Exercise 2: Division of Complex Numbers
**Problem:**
Perform the division:
$$
w = \frac{2 + 3i}{1 - 2i}
$$

**Solution:**
1. Multiply the numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator, i.e. $1 + 2i$:
   $$
   w = \frac{(2 + 3i)(1 + 2i)}{(1 - 2i)(1 + 2i)}
   $$
2. Expand the numerator:
   $$
   (2 + 3i)(1 + 2i) = 2(1) + 2(2i) + 3i(1) + 3i(2i) = 2 + 4i + 3i - 6 = -4 + 7i
   $$
3. Expand the denominator (sum of squares):
   $$
   (1 - 2i)(1 + 2i) = 1^2 + 2^2 = 1 + 4 = 5
   $$
4. Divide the terms:
   $$
   w = \frac{-4 + 7i}{5} = -\frac{4}{5} + \frac{7}{5}i
   $$

### Exercise 3: Finding the Modulus and Conjugate
**Problem:**
Find the modulus and conjugate of $z = -3 + 4i$.

**Solution:**
1. **Conjugate:** Change the sign of the imaginary part:
   $$
   \bar{z} = -3 - 4i
   $$
2. **Modulus:** Apply the modulus formula:
   $$
   |z| = \sqrt{(-3)^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5
   $$

### Exercise 4: Factoring a Trinomial with $\Delta < 0$
**Problem:**
Solve the equation $x^2 - x + 5 = 0$ in the set of complex numbers $\mathbb{C}$ and write the corresponding factorization.

**Solution:**
1. Compute the discriminant of the quadratic equation:
   $$
   \Delta = (-1)^2 - 4(1)(5) = 1 - 20 = -19
   $$
2. Since the discriminant is negative, the roots are complex conjugate numbers:
   $$
   x_{1,2} = \frac{-(-1) \pm i\sqrt{19}}{2(1)} = \frac{1 \pm i\sqrt{19}}{2} = \frac{1}{2} \pm \frac{\sqrt{19}}{2}i
   $$
3. The factorization of the trinomial $x^2 - x + 5$ is:
   $$
   x^2 - x + 5 = \left( x - \left(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{19}}{2}i\right) \right) \left( x - \left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{\sqrt{19}}{2}i\right) \right)
   $$

### Exercise 5: Conversion to Trigonometric Form
**Problem:**
Convert $z = -\sqrt{3} + i$ to trigonometric (polar) form.

**Solution:**
1. Compute the modulus $r$:
   $$
   r = \sqrt{(-\sqrt{3})^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{3 + 1} = \sqrt{4} = 2
   $$
2. Determine the argument $\theta$ from the relations:
   $$
   \cos(\theta) = \frac{x}{r} = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \quad \sin(\theta) = \frac{y}{r} = \frac{1}{2}
   $$
3. Since cosine is negative and sine is positive, the angle $\theta$ is in the second quadrant.
   - The reference angle in the first quadrant is $\frac{\pi}{6}$.
   - Therefore, the angle in the second quadrant is: $\theta = \pi - \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{5\pi}{6}$.
4. Write the trigonometric form:
   $$
   z = 2 \left( \cos\left(\frac{5\pi}{6}\right) + i \sin\left(\frac{5\pi}{6}\right) \right)
   $$

### Exercise 6: Conversion from Trigonometric to Cartesian Form
**Problem:**
Convert the following complex number to Cartesian form:
$$
z = 4 \left( \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right) + i \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right) \right)
$$

**Solution:**
1. Compute the trigonometric values for the angle $\frac{\pi}{3}$ ($60^\circ$):
   - $\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right) = \frac{1}{2}$
   - $\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
2. Substitute into the formula and apply the distributive property:
   $$
   z = 4 \left( \frac{1}{2} + i \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right) = 4 \cdot \frac{1}{2} + i \left( 4 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right) = 2 + 2\sqrt{3}i
   $$
3. The Cartesian form is $z = 2 + 2\sqrt{3}i$.

### Exercise 7: Computing a Power Using De Moivre's Theorem
**Problem:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> Compute the power $(1 + i)^{10}$ using De Moivre's Theorem.

**Solution:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> 1. First convert $w = 1 + i$ to polar form:
>    - Modulus: $r = \sqrt{1^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{2}$.
>    - Argument: $\cos(\theta) = 1/\sqrt{2}$, $\sin(\theta) = 1/\sqrt{2}$ (first quadrant) $\implies \theta = \frac{\pi}{4}$.
>    - Therefore $w = \sqrt{2} \left( \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right) + i \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right) \right)$.
> 2. Apply De Moivre's Theorem for exponent $n = 10$:
>    $$
>    w^{10} = (\sqrt{2})^{10} \left( \cos\left(10 \cdot \frac{\pi}{4}\right) + i \sin\left(10 \cdot \frac{\pi}{4}\right) \right)
>    $$
> 3. Compute the individual terms:
>    - $(\sqrt{2})^{10} = (2^{1/2})^{10} = 2^5 = 32$.
>    - The angle is $\frac{10\pi}{4} = \frac{5\pi}{2} = 2\pi + \frac{\pi}{2}$, which coincides with the angle $\frac{\pi}{2}$ due to periodicity.
> 4. Substitute the values:
>    - $\cos(5\pi/2) = \cos(\pi/2) = 0$
>    - $\sin(5\pi/2) = \sin(\pi/2) = 1$
> 5. Therefore:
>    $$
>    w^{10} = 32 (0 + i \cdot 1) = 32i
>    $$

### Exercise 8: Handling Complex Numbers in Octave
**Problem:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> Write Octave code that defines two complex numbers $z_1 = 2+3i$ and $z_2 = 1-2i$, and computes their product, quotient, real part, and modulus.

**Solution:**
> **[Supplementary]**
```octave
z1 = 2 + 3i;
z2 = 1 - 2i;

% Operations
z_prod = z1 * z2;
z_div = z1 / z2;

% Display results
disp('Product of z1 and z2:');

disp('Quotient of z1 and z2:');
disp(z_div);
disp('Real part of z1:');
disp(real(z1));
disp('Modulus of z1:');
disp(abs(z1));
```

## Exam Tip: Determining the Argument of a Complex Number

One of the most frequent mistakes in exams is the incorrect computation of the argument $\theta$ for the polar form.
- **Wrong Rule:** $\theta = \tan^{-1}(y/x)$ applied blindly. This gives the correct angle **only** when the complex number is in the 1st or 4th quadrant ($x > 0$).
- **Correct Methodology:**
  1. Identify which quadrant the point $(x, y)$ belongs to:
     - 1st quadrant ($x>0, y>0$): $\theta = \tan^{-1}(y/x)$.
     - 2nd quadrant ($x<0, y>0$): $\theta = \pi - \tan^{-1}(|y/x|)$.
     - 3rd quadrant ($x<0, y<0$): $\theta = \pi + \tan^{-1}(|y/x|)$ or $-\pi + \tan^{-1}(|y/x|)$.
     - 4th quadrant ($x>0, y<0$): $\theta = -\tan^{-1}(|y/x|)$.
  Always sketch the point on the Argand plane to visually confirm that the angle you found is reasonable!
