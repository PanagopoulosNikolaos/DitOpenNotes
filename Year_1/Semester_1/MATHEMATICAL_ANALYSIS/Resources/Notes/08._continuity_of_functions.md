# Continuity of Functions

Study of the concept of continuity of a function at a point and on an interval, continuity conditions, categories of continuous functions, Bolzano's Theorem, and the Intermediate Value Theorem.

---

## 1. Continuity at a Point and on an Interval

Continuity expresses the property of a function of not having sudden "jumps" or breaks in its graph.

### Definition of Continuity at a Point
A function $f$ is continuous at a point $x_0$ of its domain if and only if the following three conditions are satisfied:
1. $f(x)$ is defined at $x_0$ (i.e., $x_0 \in D(f)$).
2. The limit of $f(x)$ as $x$ tends to $x_0$ exists: $\lim_{x \to x_0} f(x) \in \mathbb{R}$.
3. The limit equals the value of the function at the point:
   $$
   \lim_{x \to x_0} f(x) = f(x_0)
   $$

### Categories of Discontinuities
If a function is not continuous at $x_0$, it exhibits a discontinuity:
- **Removable Discontinuity:** The limit $\lim_{x \to x_0} f(x)$ exists, but $\lim_{x \to x_0} f(x) \neq f(x_0)$.
- **Jump Discontinuity:** The one-sided limits exist but are different from each other.
- **Infinite Discontinuity:** At least one of the one-sided limits tends to $\pm\infty$.

### Continuity on a Closed Interval
A function $f$ is continuous on a closed interval $[a, b]$ if it is continuous at every point of the open interval $(a, b)$, and additionally:
$$
\lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) = f(a) \quad \text{and} \quad \lim_{x \to b^-} f(x) = f(b)
$$

## 2. Theorems on Continuous Functions

These theorems are the most powerful tools for finding roots and analyzing values.

### Bolzano's Theorem
Let a function $f$ be:
1. Continuous on the closed interval $[a, b]$.
2. Its values at the endpoints of the interval are of opposite sign, i.e.:
   $$
   f(a) \cdot f(b) < 0
   $$
Then there exists at least one $c \in (a, b)$ such that:
$$
f(c) = 0
$$
*(Geometrically: the graph of $f$ crosses the $x$-axis at least once within the interval.)*

### Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT)
Let a function $f$ be continuous on the closed interval $[a, b]$. If $N$ is any number between $f(a)$ and $f(b)$ with $f(a) \neq f(b)$, then there exists at least one $c \in (a, b)$ such that:
$$
f(c) = N
$$

## Solved Exercises

### Exercise 1: Continuity Check of a Polynomial
**Problem:**
Show that the function $f(x) = x^2$ is continuous at $x_0 = 3$.

**Solution:**
1. The value of the function at the point is:
   $$
   f(3) = 3^2 = 9
   $$
2. We compute the limit:
   $$
   \lim_{x \to 3} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 3} x^2 = 9
   $$
3. Since $\lim_{x \to 3} f(x) = f(3) = 9$, the function is continuous at $x_0 = 3$.

### Exercise 2: Finding a Parameter for Continuity
**Problem:**
Find the value of $k$ such that the function $f(x)$ is continuous at $x = 2$:
$$
f(x) = \begin{cases} 
k x^2, & x \le 2 \\
2x + k, & x > 2
\end{cases}
$$

**Solution:**
1. For $f$ to be continuous at $x = 2$, the left-hand limit, the right-hand limit, and the value $f(2)$ must all be equal.
2. We compute the value and the left-hand limit:
   $$
   f(2) = k(2^2) = 4k
   $$
   $$
   \lim_{x \to 2^-} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 2^-} (k x^2) = 4k
   $$
3. We compute the right-hand limit:
   $$
   \lim_{x \to 2^+} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 2^+} (2x + k) = 2(2) + k = 4 + k
   $$
4. We equate the limits:
   $$
   4k = 4 + k \implies 3k = 4 \implies k = \frac{4}{3}
   $$

### Exercise 3: Classifying a Discontinuity
**Problem:**
Identify the points of discontinuity of the function $f(x) = \frac{x^2 - 1}{x^2 - x}$ and classify them.

**Solution:**
1. The function is defined when the denominator $x^2 - x = x(x-1) \neq 0 \implies x \neq 0$ and $x \neq 1$.
   So, the possible points of discontinuity are $x = 0$ and $x = 1$.
2. **At $x = 1$:**
   $$
   \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x^2 - 1}{x^2 - x} = \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{(x-1)(x+1)}{x(x-1)} = \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x+1}{x} = \frac{1+1}{1} = 2
   $$
   The limit exists and is finite, but the function is not defined at $1$. This is a **removable discontinuity**.
3. **At $x = 0$:**
   - The numerator tends to $0^2 - 1 = -1$.
   - The denominator tends to $0$.
   - The limit $\lim_{x \to 0} |f(x)| = +\infty$. This is an **infinite discontinuity** (vertical asymptote $x=0$).

### Exercise 4: Existence of a Root (Bolzano)
**Problem:**
Prove that the equation $x^3 - x - 1 = 0$ has at least one real root in the interval $[1, 2]$.

**Solution:**
1. We define the function $f(x) = x^3 - x - 1$.
2. $f(x)$ is a polynomial, therefore it is continuous on the closed interval $[1, 2]$.
3. We compute the values at the endpoints:
   $$
   f(1) = 1^3 - 1 - 1 = -1 < 0
   $$
   $$
   f(2) = 2^3 - 2 - 1 = 8 - 3 = 5 > 0
   $$
4. We observe that $f(1) \cdot f(2) = (-1) \cdot 5 = -5 < 0$.
5. By Bolzano's Theorem, there exists at least one $c \in (1, 2)$ such that $f(c) = 0$, meaning the equation has at least one root in this interval.

### Exercise 5: Proof of Continuity of a Trigonometric Function
**Problem:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> Prove that the function $f(x) = \sin(x)$ is continuous at any point $x_0 \in \mathbb{R}$.

**Solution:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> 1. We want to show that $\lim_{x \to x_0} \sin(x) = \sin(x_0)$, or equivalently $\lim_{h \to 0} \sin(x_0 + h) = \sin(x_0)$.
> 2. We use the trigonometric identity:
>    $$
>    \sin(x_0 + h) - \sin(x_0) = 2 \sin\left(\frac{h}{2}\right) \cos\left(x_0 + \frac{h}{2}\right)
>    $$
> 3. We take the limit as $h \to 0$:
>    $$
>    \lim_{h \to 0} \left| \sin(x_0 + h) - \sin(x_0) \right| = \lim_{h \to 0} 2 \left| \sin\left(\frac{h}{2}\right) \right| \left| \cos\left(x_0 + \frac{h}{2}\right) \right|
>    $$
> 4. Since $|\cos(\theta)| \le 1$ and $|\sin(\theta)| \le |\theta|$:
>    $$
>    0 \le 2 \left| \sin\left(\frac{h}{2}\right) \right| \left| \cos\left(x_0 + \frac{h}{2}\right)\right| \le 2 \cdot \frac{|h|}{2} \cdot 1 = |h|
>    $$
> 5. As $h \to 0$, $|h| \to 0$. By the Squeeze Theorem:
>    $$
>    \lim_{h \to 0} (\sin(x_0 + h) - \sin(x_0)) = 0 \implies \lim_{h \to 0} \sin(x_0 + h) = \sin(x_0)
>    $$
>    Therefore, the function is continuous.

### Exercise 6: Existence of a Square Root (IVT)
**Problem:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> Using the Intermediate Value Theorem, prove that there exists a positive number $c$ such that $c^2 = 2$ (existence of $\sqrt{2}$).

**Solution:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> 1. We define the function $f(x) = x^2$ on the interval $[1, 2]$.
> 2. $f(x) = x^2$ is continuous on $[1, 2]$ as a polynomial.
> 3. We compute the values at the endpoints:
>    $$
>    f(1) = 1^2 = 1, \quad f(2) = 2^2 = 4
>    $$
> 4. The number $N = 2$ lies between $f(1)=1$ and $f(2)=4$ (i.e., $1 < 2 < 4$).
> 5. By the Intermediate Value Theorem, there exists at least one number $c \in (1, 2)$ such that:
>    $$
>    f(c) = 2 \implies c^2 = 2
>    $$
>    This number $c$ is $\sqrt{2}$.

### Exercise 7: Existence of a Root of a Mixed Equation
**Problem:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> Show that the equation $x \sin(x) - \cos(x) = 0$ has at least one solution in the interval $[0, \pi]$.

**Solution:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> 1. We define the function $g(x) = x \sin(x) - \cos(x)$.
> 2. $g(x)$ is continuous on $[0, \pi]$ as a sum and product of continuous functions ($x$, $\sin(x)$, $\cos(x)$).
> 3. We compute the values at the endpoints:
>    $$
>    g(0) = 0 \cdot \sin(0) - \cos(0) = 0 - 1 = -1 < 0
>    $$
>    $$
>    g(\pi) = \pi \cdot \sin(\pi) - \cos(\pi) = \pi \cdot 0 - (-1) = 1 > 0
>    $$
> 4. Since $g(0) \cdot g(\pi) = (-1) \cdot 1 = -1 < 0$.
> 5. By Bolzano's Theorem, there exists at least one $c \in (0, \pi)$ such that $g(c) = 0$, meaning the equation has at least one solution in $(0, \pi)$.

### Exercise 8: Continuity of a Trigonometric Function with Divided Argument
**Problem:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> Investigate whether the following function is continuous at $x = 0$:
> $$
> f(x) = \begin{cases} 
> \frac{\sin(x)}{x}, & x \neq 0 \\
> 1, & x = 0
> \end{cases}
> $$

**Solution:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> 1. We check the three continuity conditions at $x_0 = 0$:
>    - The function is defined at $0$ with value $f(0) = 1$.
>    - We compute the limit as $x \to 0$:
>      $$
>      \lim_{x \to 0} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(x)}{x}
>      $$
>    - From basic trigonometric limit theory, we know that:
>      $$
>      \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(x)}{x} = 1
>      $$
>    - We compare the limit with the value:
>      $$
>      \lim_{x \to 0} f(x) = f(0) = 1
>      $$
> 2. Since all conditions are satisfied, the function $f(x)$ is continuous at $x = 0$.

## Exam Tip: Checking Bolzano's Conditions

In exams, applying Bolzano's Theorem is one of the most common questions. To receive full marks, you must explicitly state and justify both conditions:
1. **Continuity:** Always state that the function is continuous on the closed interval $[a, b]$ as an operation of continuous functions. If the interval is open, the theorem does not apply directly!
2. **Opposite-Sign Values:** Compute $f(a)$ and $f(b)$ explicitly and show that their product is negative ($< 0$).
*Caution:* If the equation is not in the form $f(x) = 0$, move all terms to the left-hand side before defining the function.
