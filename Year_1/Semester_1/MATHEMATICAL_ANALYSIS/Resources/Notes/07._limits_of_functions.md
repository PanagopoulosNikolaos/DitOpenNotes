# Limits of Functions

Introduction to differential calculus, definition of the limit of a function, one-sided limits, limit computation techniques, the Squeeze Theorem, infinite limits (vertical asymptotes), limits at infinity (horizontal asymptotes), and finding limits in Octave.

---

## 1. Definition of a Limit and One-Sided Limits

The limit describes the behavior of a function as the independent variable approaches a specific point.

### Mathematical Definition of a Limit ($\epsilon$-$\delta$ Definition)
Let $f(x)$ be a function defined on an open interval around $x_0$, with possible exclusion of $x_0$ itself. We write:
$$
\lim_{x \to x_0} f(x) = L
$$
if for every $\epsilon > 0$ there exists a number $\delta > 0$ such that:
$$
\text{if } 0 < |x - x_0| < \delta, \quad \text{then } |f(x) - L| < \epsilon
$$

### One-Sided Limits
- **Left-hand limit:**
  $$
  \lim_{x \to x_0^-} f(x) = L_1
  $$
  $x$ approaches $x_0$ with values smaller than $x_0$ ($x < x_0$).
- **Right-hand limit:**
  $$
  \lim_{x \to x_0^+} f(x) = L_2
  $$
  $x$ approaches $x_0$ with values greater than $x_0$ ($x > x_0$).

### Existence of a Limit
The limit of a function $f(x)$ at $x_0$ exists if and only if both one-sided limits exist and are equal:
$$
\lim_{x \to x_0} f(x) = L \iff \lim_{x \to x_0^-} f(x) = \lim_{x \to x_0^+} f(x) = L
$$

## 2. Limit Computation Techniques

When direct substitution yields an indeterminate form (e.g., $0/0$), we use the following methods:
1. **Factoring and Simplification:** Used for rational functions to cancel the common factor $(x - x_0)$ that causes the zero.
2. **Using Conjugate Expressions:** Used for functions with radicals by multiplying numerator and denominator by the conjugate expression to eliminate the indeterminacy.

### Squeeze Theorem
Let $f(x), g(x), h(x)$ be three functions such that $g(x) \le f(x) \le h(x)$ on an open interval around $x_0$ (possibly excluding $x_0$). If:
$$
\lim_{x \to x_0} g(x) = \lim_{x \to x_0} h(x) = L
$$
then also:
$$
\lim_{x \to x_0} f(x) = L
$$

### Basic Trigonometric Limits
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(x)}{x} = 1, \quad \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\cos(x) - 1}{x} = 0
$$

## 3. Limits at Infinity and Asymptotes

### Vertical Asymptote
The line $x = x_0$ is called a vertical asymptote of the graph of $f(x)$ if at least one of the one-sided limits at $x_0$ is $+\infty$ or $-\infty$.

### Horizontal Asymptote
The line $y = L$ is called a horizontal asymptote of the graph of $f(x)$ if:
$$
\lim_{x \to \infty} f(x) = L \quad \text{or} \quad \lim_{x \to -\infty} f(x) = L
$$

### Limits of Polynomial and Rational Functions at Infinity
- For the polynomial $P(x) = a_n x^n + \dots + a_0$:
  $$
  \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} P(x) = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} (a_n x^n)
  $$
- For the rational function $f(x) = \frac{a_n x^n + \dots}{b_m x^m + \dots}$:
  $$
  \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} f(x) = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{a_n x^n}{b_m x^m}
  $$

---

## 4. Finding Limits in Octave

In Octave (with the `symbolic` package), we can compute limits using the `limit` command.
```octave
pkg load symbolic
syms x
f = (x^2 - 4) / (x - 2);
limit(f, x, 2)
```

## Solved Exercises

### Exercise 1: Limit of a Rational Function by Factoring
**Problem:**
Compute the limit:
$$
\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^2 - 4}{x - 2}
$$

**Solution:**
1. Direct substitution of $x=2$ yields the indeterminate form $\frac{0}{0}$.
2. We factor the numerator as a difference of squares:
   $$
   x^2 - 4 = (x - 2)(x + 2)
   $$
3. We substitute into the limit and cancel the $(x-2)$ term, since for $x \to 2$ we have $x \neq 2$:
   $$
   \lim_{x \to 2} \frac{(x - 2)(x + 2)}{x - 2} = \lim_{x \to 2} (x + 2)
   $$
4. We compute the limit by substitution:
   $$
   2 + 2 = 4
   $$

### Exercise 2: Limit with Radicals (Conjugate Expression)
**Problem:**
Compute the limit:
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sqrt{x^2 + 9} - 3}{x^2}
$$

**Solution:**
1. Direct substitution yields $\frac{0}{0}$.
2. We multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the numerator $\sqrt{x^2+9} + 3$:
   $$
   \frac{(\sqrt{x^2+9} - 3)(\sqrt{x^2+9} + 3)}{x^2(\sqrt{x^2+9} + 3)} = \frac{(x^2 + 9) - 9}{x^2(\sqrt{x^2+9} + 3)} = \frac{x^2}{x^2(\sqrt{x^2+9} + 3)}
   $$
3. We simplify $x^2$:
   $$
   \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 + 9} + 3}
   $$
4. We substitute $x=0$:
   $$
   \frac{1}{\sqrt{0^2 + 9} + 3} = \frac{1}{3 + 3} = \frac{1}{6}
   $$

### Exercise 3: Application of the Squeeze Theorem
**Problem:**
Prove that:
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} x^2 \sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = 0
$$

**Solution:**
1. We know that the sine function is bounded for any real argument:
   $$
   -1 \le \sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) \le 1, \quad \forall x \neq 0
   $$
2. We multiply all parts by $x^2$ (which is always $\ge 0$):
   $$
   -x^2 \le x^2 \sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) \le x^2
   $$
3. We compute the limits of the outer functions as $x \to 0$:
   $$
   \lim_{x \to 0} (-x^2) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \lim_{x \to 0} (x^2) = 0
   $$
4. By the Squeeze Theorem, we have:
   $$
   \lim_{x \to 0} x^2 \sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = 0
   $$

### Exercise 4: Finding Asymptotes
**Problem:**
Find the vertical and horizontal asymptotes of the function:
$$
f(x) = \frac{2x + 3}{x - 1}
$$

**Solution:**
1. **Vertical Asymptotes:**
   - The denominator is zero for $x = 1$.
   - We compute the one-sided limits at $x = 1$:
     - For $x \to 1^+$: the denominator $x-1 \to 0^+$ while the numerator $2x+3 \to 5 > 0$. So $\lim_{x \to 1^+} f(x) = +\infty$.
     - For $x \to 1^-$: the denominator $x-1 \to 0^-$ while the numerator $2x+3 \to 5$. So $\lim_{x \to 1^-} f(x) = -\infty$.
   - Therefore, the line $x = 1$ is a vertical asymptote.
2. **Horizontal Asymptotes:**
   - We compute the limit at infinity:
     $$
     \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{2x + 3}{x - 1} = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{2x}{x} = 2
     $$
   - Therefore, the line $y = 2$ is a horizontal asymptote.

### Exercise 5: Limit at Infinity of a Rational Function
**Problem:**
Compute the limit:
$$
\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{3x^3 - 2x^2 + 1}{5x^3 + 7x - 4}
$$

**Solution:**
1. We keep only the highest-degree terms of the numerator and denominator:
   $$
   \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{3x^3 - 2x^2 + 1}{5x^3 + 7x - 4} = \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{3x^3}{5x^3}
   $$
2. We simplify $x^3$:
   $$
   \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{3}{5} = \frac{3}{5}
   $$

### Exercise 6: One-Sided Limits with Absolute Value
**Problem:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> Investigate whether the limit of the function exists at $x = 1$:
> $$
> f(x) = \frac{x - 1}{|x - 1|}
> $$

**Solution:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> 1. We analyze the absolute value:
>    - If $x > 1$, then $|x - 1| = x - 1$, so $f(x) = \frac{x-1}{x-1} = 1$.
>    - If $x < 1$, then $|x - 1| = -(x - 1)$, so $f(x) = \frac{x-1}{-(x-1)} = -1$.
> 2. We compute the one-sided limits:
>    - Left-hand limit: $\lim_{x \to 1^-} f(x) = -1$
>    - Right-hand limit: $\lim_{x \to 1^+} f(x) = 1$
> 3. Since the one-sided limits are not equal ($-1 \neq 1$), the limit $\lim_{x \to 1} f(x)$ does not exist.

### Exercise 7: Trigonometric Limit with Change of Variable
**Problem:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> Compute the limit:
> $$
> \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(5x)}{\sin(2x)}
> $$

**Solution:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> 1. We divide numerator and denominator by $x$:
>    $$
>    \frac{\sin(5x)}{\sin(2x)} = \frac{\frac{\sin(5x)}{x}}{\frac{\sin(2x)}{x}}
>    $$
> 2. We multiply appropriately to produce the basic limits $\frac{\sin(u)}{u}$:
>    $$
>    \frac{5 \cdot \frac{\sin(5x)}{5x}}{2 \cdot \frac{\sin(2x)}{2x}}
>    $$
> 3. As $x \to 0$, we have $5x \to 0$ and $2x \to 0$. Therefore:
>    $$
>    \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(5x)}{5x} = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(2x)}{2x} = 1
>    $$
> 4. We substitute the limits:
>    $$
>    \frac{5 \cdot 1}{2 \cdot 1} = \frac{5}{2}
>    $$

### Exercise 8: Symbolic Limit Computation in Octave
**Problem:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> Write Octave code to compute the limit $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos(x)}{x^2}$.

**Solution:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> The Octave code using the symbolic package is:
```octave
pkg load symbolic
syms x
f = (1 - cos(x)) / x^2;
limit_value = limit(f, x, 0);
disp('The limit value is:');
disp(limit_value);
```
*(Expected result: `1/2`)*

## Exam Tip: Indeterminacy and Methodology

When encountering limits at infinity with radicals of the form $\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} (\sqrt{x^2+ax} - x)$, the highest-degree term method directly may lead to $\infty - \infty$ (indeterminacy).
- **Rule:** Always multiply by the conjugate expression to eliminate the difference of radicals.
- **Watch the sign:** When factoring $x^2$ out of the square root as a common factor, remember that $\sqrt{x^2} = |x|$. If $x \to -\infty$, then $\sqrt{x^2} = -x$. If you forget the minus sign, you will get the wrong answer!
