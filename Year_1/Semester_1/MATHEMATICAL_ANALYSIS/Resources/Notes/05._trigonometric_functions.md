# Trigonometric Functions

Study of trigonometric functions (sine, cosine, tangent, cotangent, secant, cosecant), measurement of angles in degrees and radians, basic identities, periodicity and symmetries, as well as solving trigonometric equations.

---

## 1. Angle Measurement and the Trigonometric Circle

An angle can be measured in degrees or radians.

### Radians
A radian is defined as the angle formed at the center of a circle by an arc with length equal to the radius of the circle.
- Since the circumference of a circle is $2\pi r$, a full rotation ($360^\circ$) equals $2\pi$ radians.
- **Conversion from Degrees ($d$) to Radians ($r$):**
  $$
  r = d \cdot \frac{\pi}{180}
  $$
- **Conversion from Radians ($r$) to Degrees ($d$):**
  $$
  d = r \cdot \frac{180}{\pi}
  $$

### The Trigonometric Circle
It is a circle with center at the origin $(0,0)$ and radius $R = 1$.
For any angle $\theta$ (in radians) formed by the terminal side of an angle with the positive $Ox$ semi-axis:
- The **cosine** ($\cos\theta$) is the abscissa ($x$) of the intersection point of the angle with the circle.
- The **sine** ($\sin\theta$) is the ordinate ($y$) of the intersection point of the angle with the circle.

---

## 2. The Six Trigonometric Functions

Based on sine and cosine, the remaining four trigonometric functions are defined:

| Function | Formula | Domain | Period |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sine | $f(x) = \sin(x)$ | $\mathbb{R}$ | $2\pi$ |
| Cosine | $f(x) = \cos(x)$ | $\mathbb{R}$ | $2\pi$ |
| Tangent | $f(x) = \tan(x) = \frac{\sin(x)}{\cos(x)}$ | $x \neq k\pi + \frac{\pi}{2}, \quad k \in \mathbb{Z}$ | $\pi$ |
| Cotangent | $f(x) = \cot(x) = \frac{\cos(x)}{\sin(x)}$ | $x \neq k\pi, \quad k \in \mathbb{Z}$ | $\pi$ |
| Secant | $f(x) = \sec(x) = \frac{1}{\cos(x)}$ | $x \neq k\pi + \frac{\pi}{2}, \quad k \in \mathbb{Z}$ | $2\pi$ |
| Cosecant | $f(x) = \csc(x) = \frac{1}{\sin(x)}$ | $x \neq k\pi, \quad k \in \mathbb{Z}$ | $2\pi$ |

---

## 3. Basic Identities and Symmetries

### Fundamental Trigonometric Identity
For every real number $x$:
$$
\sin^2(x) + \cos^2(x) = 1
$$
Dividing by $\cos^2(x)$ or $\sin^2(x)$ respectively yields:
$$
1 + \tan^2(x) = \sec^2(x), \quad 1 + \cot^2(x) = \csc^2(x)
$$

### Symmetries and Periodicity
- **Even/Odd behavior:**
  $$
  \sin(-x) = -\sin(x) \quad (\text{odd})
  $$
  $$
  \cos(-x) = \cos(x) \quad (\text{even})
  $$
  $$
  \tan(-x) = -\tan(x) \quad (\text{odd})
  $$
- **Relationship between sine and cosine (complementary angles):**
  $$
  \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - x\right) = \cos(x), \quad \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - x\right) = \sin(x)
  $$

---

## 4. Trigonometric Equations

The basic solution forms are:
1. $\sin(x) = \sin(\theta) \implies x = 2k\pi + \theta \quad \text{or} \quad x = 2k\pi + \pi - \theta, \quad k \in \mathbb{Z}$
2. $\cos(x) = \cos(\theta) \implies x = 2k\pi \pm \theta, \quad k \in \mathbb{Z}$
3. $\tan(x) = \tan(\theta) \implies x = k\pi + \theta, \quad k \in \mathbb{Z}$

## Solved Exercises

### Exercise 1: Unit Conversion of Angles
**Problem:**
a) Convert the angle $120^\circ$ to radians.
b) Convert the angle $\frac{3\pi}{4}$ radians to degrees.

**Solution:**
a) We use the conversion formula to radians:
   $$
   r = 120 \cdot \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{120}{180}\pi = \frac{2\pi}{3}\text{ rad}
   $$
b) We use the conversion formula to degrees:
   $$
   d = \frac{3\pi}{4} \cdot \frac{180}{\pi} = \frac{3 \cdot 180}{4} = 3 \cdot 45 = 135^\circ
   $$

### Exercise 2: Solving a Simple Sine Equation
**Problem:**
Solve the equation in the interval $[0, 2\pi]$:
$$
\sin(x) = \frac{1}{2}
$$

**Solution:**
1. On the trigonometric circle, the reference angle in the first quadrant whose sine equals $1/2$ is $x_0 = \frac{\pi}{6}$.
2. The general solution of the equation $\sin(x) = \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)$ is:
   $$
   x = 2k\pi + \frac{\pi}{6} \quad \text{or} \quad x = 2k\pi + \pi - \frac{\pi}{6} = 2k\pi + \frac{5\pi}{6}, \quad k \in \mathbb{Z}
   $$
3. We seek solutions in the closed interval $[0, 2\pi]$ by testing values of $k$:
   - For $k=0$: $x_1 = \frac{\pi}{6}$ and $x_2 = \frac{5\pi}{6}$. Both values belong to $[0, 2\pi]$.
   - For any other value of $k$ (e.g., $k=1$, $k=-1$), the solutions fall outside the interval $[0, 2\pi]$.
4. The required solutions are:
   $$
   S = \left\{\frac{\pi}{6}, \frac{5\pi}{6}\right\}
   $$

### Exercise 3: Cosine Equation with Multiple Argument
**Problem:**
Solve the equation in the interval $[0, 2\pi]$:
$$
\cos(2x) = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
$$

**Solution:**
1. We know that $\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$. Since the cosine is negative, the corresponding angle in the 2nd quadrant is $\pi - \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{3\pi}{4}$. Therefore:
   $$
   \cos(2x) = \cos\left(\frac{3\pi}{4}\right)
   $$
2. We apply the solution formula for cosine:
   $$
   2x = 2k\pi \pm \frac{3\pi}{4}, \quad k \in \mathbb{Z}
   $$
3. We divide by $2$ to find $x$:
   $$
   x = k\pi \pm \frac{3\pi}{8}, \quad k \in \mathbb{Z}
   $$
4. We find the solutions in the interval $[0, 2\pi]$ for various values of $k$:
   - **Branch (+):** $x = k\pi + \frac{3\pi}{8}$
     - $k=0 \implies x = \frac{3\pi}{8}$ (accepted)
     - $k=1 \implies x = \pi + \frac{3\pi}{8} = \frac{11\pi}{8}$ (accepted)
   - **Branch (-):** $x = k\pi - \frac{3\pi}{8}$
     - $k=1 \implies x = \pi - \frac{3\pi}{8} = \frac{5\pi}{8}$ (accepted)
     - $k=2 \implies x = 2\pi - \frac{3\pi}{8} = \frac{13\pi}{8}$ (accepted)
5. The solution set is:
   $$
   S = \left\{\frac{3\pi}{8}, \frac{5\pi}{8}, \frac{11\pi}{8}, \frac{13\pi}{8}\right\}
   $$

### Exercise 4: Proof of a Trigonometric Identity
**Problem:**
Prove the identity:
$$
\tan(x) + \cot(x) = \sec(x)\csc(x)
$$

**Solution:**
1. We analyze the left-hand side of the identity using the definitions of $\tan(x)$ and $\cot(x)$:
   $$
   \tan(x) + \cot(x) = \frac{\sin(x)}{\cos(x)} + \frac{\cos(x)}{\sin(x)}
   $$
2. We combine the fractions over a common denominator:
   $$
   \frac{\sin(x) \cdot \sin(x) + \cos(x) \cdot \cos(x)}{\cos(x)\sin(x)} = \frac{\sin^2(x) + \cos^2(x)}{\cos(x)\sin(x)}
   $$
3. We apply the fundamental trigonometric identity $\sin^2(x) + \cos^2(x) = 1$:
   $$
   \frac{1}{\cos(x)\sin(x)} = \frac{1}{\cos(x)} \cdot \frac{1}{\sin(x)}
   $$
4. We substitute the definitions of $\sec(x)$ and $\csc(x)$:
   $$
   \sec(x)\csc(x)
   $$
   which equals the right-hand side. The identity is proved.

### Exercise 5: Quadratic Trigonometric Equation
**Problem:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> Solve the equation:
> $$
> \sin^2(x) - 3\sin(x) + 2 = 0
> $$

**Solution:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> 1. We set $u = \sin(x)$ with the constraint $|u| \le 1$ due to the range of the sine function.
> 2. The equation becomes a quadratic trinomial:
>    $$
>    u^2 - 3u + 2 = 0
>    $$
> 3. We factor the trinomial:
>    $$
>    (u - 1)(u - 2) = 0 \implies u = 1 \quad \text{or} \quad u = 2
>    $$
> 4. We check the constraint $|u| \le 1$:
>    - The solution $u = 1$ is accepted.
>    - The solution $u = 2$ is rejected as impossible.
> 5. We return to the variable $x$:
>    $$
>    \sin(x) = 1 \implies x = 2k\pi + \frac{\pi}{2}, \quad k \in \mathbb{Z}
>    $$

### Exercise 6: Finding the Domain
**Problem:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> Determine the domain of the function $f(x) = \frac{1}{\cos(x) - 1/2}$.

**Solution:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> 1. The function is defined for all values of $x$ except those that make the denominator zero:
>    $$
>    \cos(x) - \frac{1}{2} = 0 \implies \cos(x) = \frac{1}{2}
>    $$
> 2. We know that $\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right) = \frac{1}{2}$. Therefore, the solutions of the equation are:
>    $$
>    x = 2k\pi \pm \frac{\pi}{3}, \quad k \in \mathbb{Z}
>    $$
> 3. Consequently, the domain is:
>    $$
>    D(f) = \mathbb{R} \setminus \left\{2k\pi \pm \frac{\pi}{3} \;\middle|\; k \in \mathbb{Z}\right\}
>    $$

### Exercise 7: Amplitude and Period of a Trigonometric Function
**Problem:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> Find the amplitude and period of the function:
> $$
> y = 3 \sin\left(2x - \frac{\pi}{3}\right)
> $$

**Solution:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> 1. The general form of a sinusoidal function is $y = A \sin(B(x - C)) + D$.
> 2. Here we have $A = 3$, so the amplitude of the function is:
>    $$
>    \text{Amplitude} = |A| = 3
>    $$
> 3. The frequency coefficient is the coefficient of $x$, namely $B = 2$.
> 4. The period $T$ of the function is calculated from the formula:
>    $$
>    T = \frac{2\pi}{|B|} = \frac{2\pi}{2} = \pi
>    $$

### Exercise 8: Plotting Sine and Cosine in Octave
**Problem:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> Write an Octave program that plots the functions $\sin(x)$ and $\cos(x)$ in the same window on the interval $[-\pi, \pi]$ with different colors and a legend.

**Solution:**
> **[Supplementary]**
```octave
% Definition of the interval x
x = -pi:0.02:pi;

% Calculation of values
y1 = sin(x);
y2 = cos(x);

% Plotting the two curves
figure;
plot(x, y1, 'r-', 'LineWidth', 1.5); hold on;
plot(x, y2, 'b--', 'LineWidth', 1.5);
hold off;

% Formatting the plot
grid on;
xlabel('x (rad)');
ylabel('y');
legend('y = sin(x)', 'y = cos(x)', 'Location', 'South');
title('Comparison of Sine and Cosine Functions');
```

## Exam Tip: Solving Trigonometric Equations

In exams, when solving equations of the form $\cos(Ax + B) = C$ or $\sin(Ax + B) = C$:
1. Do not try to expand $Ax + B$. Treat the entire argument as a single angle $\Phi$.
2. Find the general solutions for the angle $\Phi$ (including the term $2k\pi$).
3. At the end, solve the first-degree equation for $x$ by dividing by $A$. This will ensure you find all solutions, since the period of the function changes to $\frac{2\pi}{A}$. If you divided at the end without the $2k\pi$ term, you would lose most of the solutions!
