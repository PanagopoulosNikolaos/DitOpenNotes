# Indefinite Integrals

Study of integral calculus, the concept of the antiderivative (primitive function), the definition of the indefinite integral, the table of basic integrals, the integration by parts method, the substitution method, and the use of Octave.

---

## 1. Antiderivative and Indefinite Integral

Integration is the inverse process of differentiation.

### Definition of Antiderivative
A function $F(x)$ is called an antiderivative (or primitive) of $f(x)$ on an interval $I$ if:
$$
F'(x) = f(x)
$$
for every $x \in I$.
- **Theorem:** If $F$ is an antiderivative of $f$, then the general form of all antiderivatives of $f$ is $F(x) + C$, where $C$ is an arbitrary real constant. Geometrically, antiderivatives are vertical translations of one another.

### Definition of Indefinite Integral
The indefinite integral of $f(x)$ denotes the set of all its antiderivatives and is written as:
$$
\int f(x) \, dx = F(x) + C
$$

## 2. Table of Basic Integrals

| Function $f(x)$ | Indefinite Integral $\int f(x) \, dx$ | Restrictions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $k$ (constant) | $kx + C$ | — |
| $x^n$ | $\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} + C$ | $n \neq -1$ |
| $\frac{1}{x}$ | $\ln|x| + C$ | $x \neq 0$ |
| $e^x$ | $e^x + C$ | — |
| $a^x$ | $\frac{a^x}{\ln(a)} + C$ | $a > 0, a \neq 1$ |
| $\sin(x)$ | $-\cos(x) + C$ | — |
| $\cos(x)$ | $\sin(x) + C$ | — |
| $\sec^2(x)$ | $\tan(x) + C$ | $x \neq k\pi + \frac{\pi}{2}$ |
| $\csc^2(x)$ | $-\cot(x) + C$ | $x \neq k\pi$ |

## 3. Integration Methods

### Integration by Parts
Derived from the product rule for differentiation:
$$
\int f(x) g'(x) \, dx = f(x)g(x) - \int f'(x) g(x) \, dx
$$
Or in differential form:
$$
\int u \, dv = uv - \int v \, du
$$

### Substitution Rule
Derived from the chain rule for composite functions:
$$
\int f(g(x)) g'(x) \, dx = \int f(u) \, du
$$
setting $u = g(x)$ and $du = g'(x) dx$.

## 4. Integration in Octave

In Octave, we use the `int` command from the symbolic package:
```octave
pkg load symbolic
syms x
f = x^2 * log(x);
int(f, x)
```

## Solved Exercises

### Exercise 1: Basic Integral with Linearity Properties
**Problem:**
Compute the integral:
$$
\int (3x^5 - 2\cos(x) + e^x) \, dx
$$

**Solution:**
1. Separate the integral using linearity:
   $$
   3 \int x^5 \, dx - 2 \int \cos(x) \, dx + \int e^x \, dx
   $$
2. Apply the basic integration formulas for each term individually:
   - $\int x^5 \, dx = \frac{x^6}{6}$
   - $\int \cos(x) \, dx = \sin(x)$
   - $\int e^x \, dx = e^x$
3. Substitute and add the constant of integration $C$:
   $$
   3\left(\frac{x^6}{6}\right) - 2\sin(x) + e^x + C = \frac{x^6}{2} - 2\sin(x) + e^x + C
   $$

### Exercise 2: Integration by Parts
**Problem:**
Compute the integral:
$$
\int x e^x \, dx
$$

**Solution:**
1. Select the functions $u$ and $dv$ according to the LIATE priority rule (polynomial vs. exponential):
   - Set $u = x \implies du = dx$
   - Set $dv = e^x dx \implies v = e^x$
2. Apply the formula $\int u \, dv = uv - \int v \, du$:
   $$
   \int x e^x \, dx = x e^x - \int e^x \, dx
   $$
3. Compute the remaining integral:
   $$
   x e^x - e^x + C
   $$
4. Factor out $e^x$:
   $$
   e^x(x - 1) + C
   $$

### Exercise 3: Integration by Substitution
**Problem:**
Compute the integral:
$$
\int x \cos(x^2 + 1) \, dx
$$

**Solution:**
1. Notice that the derivative of $x^2 + 1$ is $2x$, which appears almost as a factor in the integral.
2. Set:
   $$
   u = x^2 + 1 \implies du = 2x \, dx \implies x \, dx = \frac{du}{2}
   $$
3. Substitute into the integral:
   $$
   \int \cos(x^2 + 1) \cdot (x \, dx) = \int \cos(u) \frac{du}{2} = \frac{1}{2} \int \cos(u) \, du
   $$
4. Integrate with respect to $u$:
   $$
   \frac{1}{2} \sin(u) + C
   $$
5. Return to the original variable $x$:
   $$
   \frac{1}{2} \sin(x^2 + 1) + C
   $$

### Exercise 4: Integration of $\ln(x)$
**Problem:**
Compute the integral:
$$
\int \ln(x) \, dx
$$

**Solution:**
1. Although the logarithm is a simple function, there is no direct formula. Use integration by parts by treating $\ln(x) = 1 \cdot \ln(x)$:
   - Set $u = \ln(x) \implies du = \frac{1}{x} dx$
   - Set $dv = 1 \, dx \implies v = x$
2. Apply the formula:
   $$
   \int \ln(x) \, dx = x \ln(x) - \int x \left(\frac{1}{x}\right) \, dx
   $$
3. Simplify the term inside the integral:
   $$
   x \ln(x) - \int 1 \, dx = x \ln(x) - x + C
   $$
4. Factor out $x$:
   $$
   x(\ln(x) - 1) + C
   $$

### Exercise 5: Substitution with Fractional Form
**Problem:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> Compute the integral:
> $$
> \int \frac{x^2}{x^3 + 5} \, dx
> $$

**Solution:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> 1. Set the denominator as the new variable:
>    $$
>    u = x^3 + 5 \implies du = 3x^2 \, dx \implies x^2 \, dx = \frac{du}{3}
>    $$
> 2. Substitute into the integral:
>    $$
>    \int \frac{1}{x^3 + 5} \cdot (x^2 \, dx) = \int \frac{1}{u} \cdot \frac{du}{3} = \frac{1}{3} \int \frac{1}{u} \, du
>    $$
> 3. Integrate:
>    $$
>    \frac{1}{3} \ln|u| + C
>    $$
> 4. Substitute back $u$:
>    $$
>    \frac{1}{3} \ln|x^3 + 5| + C
>    $$

### Exercise 6: Cyclic Integration by Parts
**Problem:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> Compute the integral:
> $$
> I = \int e^x \sin(x) \, dx
> $$

**Solution:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> 1. Apply integration by parts:
>    - Set $u = \sin(x) \implies du = \cos(x) dx$
>    - Set $dv = e^x dx \implies v = e^x$
>    $$
>    I = e^x \sin(x) - \int e^x \cos(x) \, dx
>    $$
> 2. Apply integration by parts again on the new integral $\int e^x \cos(x) \, dx$:
>    - Set $u_1 = \cos(x) \implies du_1 = -\sin(x) dx$
>    - Set $dv_1 = e^x dx \implies v_1 = e^x$
>    $$
>    \int e^x \cos(x) \, dx = e^x \cos(x) - \int e^x (-\sin(x)) \, dx = e^x \cos(x) + \int e^x \sin(x) \, dx
>    $$
> 3. Substitute the result into the original equation:
>    $$
>    I = e^x \sin(x) - [ e^x \cos(x) + I ] \implies I = e^x \sin(x) - e^x \cos(x) - I
>    $$
> 4. Move $I$ to the left side:
>    $$
>    2I = e^x(\sin(x) - \cos(x)) \implies I = \frac{e^x(\sin(x) - \cos(x))}{2} + C
>    $$

### Exercise 7: Integration by Partial Fraction Decomposition
**Problem:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> Compute the integral:
> $$
> \int \frac{1}{x^2 - 9} \, dx
> $$

**Solution:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> 1. Factor the denominator: $x^2 - 9 = (x - 3)(x + 3)$.
> 2. Perform partial fraction decomposition:
>    $$
>    \frac{1}{(x-3)(x+3)} = \frac{A}{x-3} + \frac{B}{x+3}
>    $$
> 3. Combine and equate the numerators:
>    $$
>    1 = A(x + 3) + B(x - 3)
>    $$
>    - For $x = 3$: $1 = 6A \implies A = 1/6$.
>    - For $x = -3$: $1 = -6B \implies B = -1/6$.
> 4. Substitute into the integral:
>    $$
>    \int \left( \frac{1/6}{x-3} - \frac{1/6}{x+3} \right) \, dx = \frac{1}{6} \int \frac{1}{x-3} \, dx - \frac{1}{6} \int \frac{1}{x+3} \, dx
>    $$
> 5. Integrate:
>    $$
>    \frac{1}{6} \ln|x-3| - \frac{1}{6} \ln|x+3| + C = \frac{1}{6} \ln\left| \frac{x-3}{x+3} \right| + C
>    $$

### Exercise 8: Symbolic Integration in Octave
**Problem:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> Write Octave code to compute the indefinite integral $\int \sin^2(x) \, dx$.

**Solution:**
> **[Supplementary]**

## Exam Tip: Choosing an Integration Method

In exams, choosing the right method is key.
- **Substitution:** Use it when you see a composite function $f(g(x))$ whose inner derivative $g'(x)$ appears as a factor in the rest of the integral.
- **By Parts:** Use it when you have a product of different function categories (e.g., Polynomial $\cdot$ Exponential, or Polynomial $\cdot$ Trigonometric).
- **LIATE Rule for selecting $u$:**
  1. **L**ogarithmic ($\ln x$)
  2. **I**nverse Trigonometric ($\arcsin x$)
  3. **A**lgebraic ($x^2$)
  4. **T**rigonometric ($\sin x$)
  5. **E**xponential ($e^x$)
  *Whichever function appears first in the LIATE list, define that as $u$.*
