# Functions (Part B)

The study of function composition, symmetries of graphical representations (even/odd functions), geometric transformations (translations, reflections, stretches/compressions), and piecewise functions.

---

## 1. Function Composition

Composition is a way of combining two functions, where the output of one becomes the input of the other.

### Definition of Composition
Let $f: A \to B$ and $g: B \to C$ be two functions. The composition of $g$ with $f$, denoted by $g \circ f$, is defined by:
$$
(g \circ f)(x) = g(f(x))
$$

### Domain of Composition
The domain of $g \circ f$ consists of those elements $x$ of the domain of $f$ for which the value $f(x)$ belongs to the domain of $g$:
$$
D(g \circ f) = \{x \in D(f) \mid f(x) \in D(g)\}
$$

> **[Key Insight]**
> Function composition is generally not commutative, meaning that almost always:
> $$
> g \circ f \neq f \circ g
> $$

---

## 2. Graphs and Symmetries

The symmetries of a function simplify its study and drawing.

### Even Functions
A function $f$ is called even if for every $x$ in its domain, $-x$ also belongs to the domain and the following holds:
$$
f(-x) = f(x)
$$
- **Geometric Interpretation:** The graph of an even function is symmetric with respect to the vertical axis $y'y$.

### Odd Functions
A function $f$ is called odd if for every $x$ in its domain, $-x$ also belongs to the domain and the following holds:
$$
f(-x) = -f(x)
$$
- **Geometric Interpretation:** The graph of an odd function is symmetric with respect to the origin $(0,0)$.

---

## 3. Graphical Transformations

From the graph of $y = f(x)$, we can draw related functions through geometric transformations:

### Translations
- **Vertical Translation:**
  - $y = f(x) + c$ ($c > 0$): Translating the graph upward by $c$ units.
  - $y = f(x) - c$ ($c > 0$): Translating the graph downward by $c$ units.
- **Horizontal Translation:**
  - $y = f(x + c)$ ($c > 0$): Translating the graph to the left by $c$ units.
  - $y = f(x - c)$ ($c > 0$): Translating the graph to the right by $c$ units.

### Reflections
- $y = -f(x)$: Reflection of the graph across the $x'x$ axis.
- $y = f(-x)$: Reflection of the graph across the $y'y$ axis.

### Stretching and Compressing
- **Vertical:**
  - $y = c \cdot f(x)$ with $c > 1$: Vertical stretching (stretching) by a factor of $c$.
  - $y = c \cdot f(x)$ with $0 < c < 1$: Vertical compression (squeezing) by a factor of $c$.
- **Horizontal:**
  - $y = f(c \cdot x)$ with $c > 1$: Horizontal compression by a factor of $c$.
  - $y = f(c \cdot x)$ with $0 < c < 1$: Horizontal stretching by a factor of $c$.

### Graph of $|f(x)|$
The graph of $y = |f(x)|$ is obtained by keeping the portions of $y = f(x)$ that lie above the $x'x$ axis (or on it) unchanged, and reflecting across the $x'x$ axis the portions that lie below the axis.

---

## 4. Piecewise Functions

A function is called piecewise when it is defined by different formulas on different subsets of its domain.
General form:
$$
f(x) = \begin{cases} 
g_1(x), & x \in I_1 \\
g_2(x), & x \in I_2
\end{cases}
$$

## Solved Exercises

### Exercise 1: Computing a Composition
**Problem:**
If $f(x) = 3x + 1$ and $g(x) = 2x - 1$, find the formula for the function $g \circ f$ and its domain.

**Solution:**
1. The domain of both $f$ and $g$ is all of $\mathbb{R}$, so $D(f) = \mathbb{R}$ and $D(g) = \mathbb{R}$.
2. Therefore, the domain of the composition is also:
   $$
   D(g \circ f) = \mathbb{R}
   $$
3. We apply the definition of composition:
   $$
   (g \circ f)(x) = g(f(x)) = g(3x + 1)
   $$
4. We substitute the argument into the formula for $g$:
   $$
   g(3x + 1) = 2(3x + 1) - 1 = 6x + 2 - 1 = 6x + 1
   $$
5. Consequently:
   $$
   (g \circ f)(x) = 6x + 1
   $$

### Exercise 2: Composition with a Radical and a Quadratic
**Problem:**
If $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$ and $g(x) = x^2 - 1$, find $g \circ f$ and its domain.

**Solution:**
1. We determine the domains of the individual functions:
   $$
   D(f) = [0, \infty), \quad D(g) = \mathbb{R}
   $$
2. We find the domain of the composition $g \circ f$:
   $$
   D(g \circ f) = \{x \in D(f) \mid f(x) \in D(g)\} = \{x \ge 0 \mid \sqrt{x} \in \mathbb{R}\} = [0, \infty)
   $$
3. We compute the formula for the composition:
   $$
   (g \circ f)(x) = g(f(x)) = g(\sqrt{x}) = (\sqrt{x})^2 - 1 = x - 1
   $$
4. Therefore, $(g \circ f)(x) = x - 1$ with domain $D(g \circ f) = [0, \infty)$.
   *(Warning: Although the expression $x-1$ is defined on all of $\mathbb{R}$, the domain of the composition is restricted by the domain of the inner function $f$.)*

### Exercise 3: Symmetry Check (Odd)
**Problem:**
Determine whether the function $f(x) = x^3 - x$ is even, odd, or neither.

**Solution:**
1. The domain of the function is $D(f) = \mathbb{R}$, which is symmetric about the origin.
2. We compute $f(-x)$:
   $$
   f(-x) = (-x)^3 - (-x) = -x^3 + x
   $$
3. We factor out the negative sign:
   $$
   f(-x) = -(x^3 - x) = -f(x)
   $$
4. Since $f(-x) = -f(x)$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$, the function is odd.

### Exercise 4: Symmetry Check (Even)
**Problem:**
Determine whether the function $f(x) = \frac{x^2}{1 + x^4}$ is even, odd, or neither.

**Solution:**
1. The domain is $D(f) = \mathbb{R}$, which is symmetric.
2. We compute $f(-x)$:
   $$
   f(-x) = \frac{(-x)^2}{1 + (-x)^4} = \frac{x^2}{1 + x^4} = f(x)
   $$
3. Since $f(-x) = f(x)$, the function is even.

### Exercise 5: Geometric Transformations
**Problem:**
The graph of $y = |x|$ is given. Describe how the graph of $y = |x - 2| + 1$ is obtained.

**Solution:**
1. We start from the basic function $f(x) = |x|$.
2. We apply a horizontal translation: the function $f(x - 2) = |x - 2|$ corresponds to a translation of the graph of $f(x)$ by $2$ units to the right.
3. We apply a vertical translation: the function $f(x - 2) + 1 = |x - 2| + 1$ corresponds to a translation of the previous graph by $1$ unit upward.
4. The vertex of the angle moves from $(0,0)$ to the point $(2,1)$.

### Exercise 6: Study of a Piecewise Function
**Problem:**
Draw the graph of the piecewise function:
$$
f(x) = \begin{cases} 
-x, & x < 0 \\
x^2, & x \ge 0
\end{cases}
$$

**Solution:**
1. For the interval $(-\infty, 0)$, the function equals the line $y = -x$. We draw the bisector of the second quadrant.
2. For the interval $[0, \infty)$, the function equals the parabola $y = x^2$. We draw the right branch of the parabola with vertex at $(0,0)$.
3. At the point $x = 0$, the branch $y = x^2$ gives the value $0$, which coincides with the limit of the branch $y = -x$ as $x$ tends to $0$ from the left. The function is continuous at $0$.

### Exercise 7: Composition of Three Functions
**Problem:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> If $f(x) = \frac{x}{x+1}$, $g(x) = x^{10}$ and $h(x) = x+3$, find the composition $f \circ g \circ h$.

**Solution:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> 1. The composition of three functions is defined as:
>    $$
>    (f \circ g \circ h)(x) = f(g(h(x)))
>    $$
> 2. We first compute the inner composition $g(h(x))$:
>    $$
>    g(h(x)) = g(x+3) = (x+3)^{10}
>    $$
> 3. Then, we apply $f$ to the result:
>    $$
>    f(g(h(x))) = f((x+3)^{10}) = \frac{(x+3)^{10}}{(x+3)^{10} + 1}
>    $$
> 4. The domain of the composition is all of $\mathbb{R}$ since the denominator $(x+3)^{10} + 1 \ge 1 > 0$ never vanishes.

### Exercise 8: Symmetry with a Logarithm
**Problem:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> Examine the symmetry of the function:
> $$
> f(x) = \ln\left(x + \sqrt{x^2 + 1}\right)
> $$

**Solution:**
> **[Supplementary]**
> 1. The domain is $D(f) = \mathbb{R}$, since $\sqrt{x^2+1} > \sqrt{x^2} = |x| \ge -x \implies x + \sqrt{x^2+1} > 0$.
> 2. We compute $f(-x)$:
>    $$
>    f(-x) = \ln\left(-x + \sqrt{(-x)^2 + 1}\right) = \ln\left(\sqrt{x^2 + 1} - x\right)
>    $$
> 3. We multiply and divide the argument by the conjugate expression $\sqrt{x^2+1} + x$:
>    $$
>    \sqrt{x^2 + 1} - x = \frac{(\sqrt{x^2 + 1} - x)(\sqrt{x^2 + 1} + x)}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1} + x} = \frac{(x^2 + 1) - x^2}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1} + x} = \frac{1}{x + \sqrt{x^2 + 1}}
>    $$
> 4. We substitute into the logarithm:
>    $$
>    f(-x) = \ln\left(\frac{1}{x + \sqrt{x^2 + 1}}\right) = -\ln\left(x + \sqrt{x^2 + 1}\right) = -f(x)
>    $$
> 5. Consequently, the function is odd.

## Exam Tip: Domain of a Composition

In exams, never simplify the formula of the composition $g(f(x))$ before finding its domain!
*Example:* If $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$ and $g(x) = x^2$, then the formula for $g(f(x))$ is $(\sqrt{x})^2 = x$. If you look only at the final result $y = x$, you would think the domain is all of $\mathbb{R}$. However, since $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$ requires $x \ge 0$, the actual domain of the composition is $[0, \infty)$.
**Rule:** First write down the restrictions of the inner function, then those of the outer function, and only then perform algebraic simplifications.
