Scenario 
Use the network provided to you to investigate the operation of PDUs. The devices are already configured. You will gather PDU information in simulation mode and answer a series of questions regarding the data you collect. 
   
Part 1: Examine an ARP Request 
Step 1: Create ARP requests by pinging 172.16.31.3 from 172.16.31.2. 
a. Click on 172.16.31.2 and open the command prompt. 
b. Enter the command arp -d to clear the ARP table. 
c. Enter simulation mode and enter the command ping 172.16.31.3. Two PDUs will be created. The ping command cannot complete the ICMP packet without knowing the destination MAC address. Thus, the PC sends an ARP broadcast frame to find the destination MAC address. 
d. Click the Capture/Forward button once. The ARP PDU moves to Switch1, while the ICMP PDU disappears, waiting for the ARP reply. Open the PDU and record the destination MAC address. Is this address listed in the table above? 
e. Click Capture/Forward to move the PDU to the next device. How many copies of the PDU did Switch1 create? 
f. What is the IP address of the device that accepted the PDU? 
g. Open the PDU and examine Layer 2. What do you observe regarding the source and destination MAC addresses? 
h. Click Capture/Forward until the PDU returns to 172.16.31.2. How many copies of the PDU did the switch create during the ARP reply? 
   
Step 2: Examine the ARP table. 
a. Note that the ICMP packet reappears. Open the PDU and examine the MAC addresses. Do the source and destination MAC addresses agree with their IP addresses? 
b. Return to Realtime and the ping completes. 
c. Click on 172.16.31.2 and enter the command arp -a. Which IP address does the MAC address entry correspond to? 
d. In general, when does an end device issue an ARP request? 
   
Part 2: Examine a Switch MAC Address Table 
Step 1: Generate additional traffic to populate the switch MAC address table. 
a. From address 172.16.31.2, enter the command ping 172.16.31.4. 
b. Click on 10.10.10.2 and open the command prompt. 
c. Enter the command ping 10.10.10.3. How many replies were sent and received? 
Step 2: Examine the MAC address table on the switches. 
a. Click on Switch1 and then on the CLI tab. Enter the command show mac-address-table. Do the entries correspond to those in the table above? 
b. Click on Switch0 and then on the CLI tab. Enter privileged EXEC mode and enter the command show mac-address-table. Do the entries correspond to those in the table above? 
c. Why are two MAC addresses associated with a single port? 
   
Part 3: Examine the ARP process in Remote Communications 
Step 1: Generate traffic to produce ARP traffic. 
a. Click on 172.16.31.2 and open the command prompt. 
b. Enter the command ping 10.10.10.1. 
c. Type arp -a. What is the IP address of the new ARP table entry? 
d. Type arp -d to clear the ARP table and switch to simulation mode. 
e. Repeat the ping to address 10.10.10.1. How many PDUs appear? 
f. Click Capture/Forward. Click on the PDU currently at Switch1. What is the target destination IP address of the ARP request? 
g. The destination IP address is not 10.10.10.1. Why? 
   
Step 2: Examine the ARP table on Router1. 
a. Switch to realtime mode. Click on Router1 and then on the CLI tab. 
b. Enter privileged EXEC mode and then type show mac-address-table. How many MAC addresses are in the table? Why? 
c. Enter the command show arp. Is there an entry for 172.16.31.2? 
d. What happens on the first ping in case the router answers the ARP request?
